# `flex-wrap: wrap-reverse` now stacks lines in the reversed direction and swaps cross-axis item alignment

**Landed:** 2026-07-27 (46abe129) — Swap the cross axis inside a wrap-reverse line, and keep an inline-flex's own items (#474)
**Doc section:** docs/html-css-support.md § [Flexbox](../../docs/html-css-support.md#flexbox)
**Verified against v0.9.6:** this note was not present in the `v0.9.6` tag's docs — confirmed genuine behavior change since 0.9.6, in scope for the next release notes.

`flex-wrap: wrap-reverse` used to reverse the lines' cross-axis *offsets* rather than the stack itself, which is the same thing only where every line has the same cross size. Lines of different sizes used to overlap — a short line drawn inside a taller one — and the container reported a height measured from the wrong end of the stack; with free cross space to distribute, the lines also stayed at the edge `align-content` had packed them against instead of the opposite one. The lines are now stacked in the reversed direction, so such a container gets taller, its lines separate, and `align-content: flex-end`/`space-between`/`space-around`/`space-evenly` place them against the mirror-image edge. The same swap now also applies *within* a line: an item that does not fill its line's cross size used to be aligned against the unswapped edge, so `align-items: flex-start` — including through the `stretch` fallback an item with a definite cross size takes — put it at the top of a row line where `wrap-reverse` had made the bottom the cross-start edge. Such an item now moves to the other end of its line. See [Forward compatibility](#forward-compatibility).
