# Empty inline-blocks rest on the baseline, and an empty positioned inline honors its vertical-align

Before, an `inline-block` with no content and a declared `height` (or `min-height`) hung below the line's baseline with its top at the line's top, and the same for one with `overflow` other than `visible`; an empty positioned inline always anchored its absolutely positioned child on the baseline whatever its own `vertical-align` said. Now the empty box's bottom margin edge rests on the baseline (CSS 2.1 §10.8.1), so its height reaches above it and the line grows to hold it, and `vertical-align: baseline|sub|super|<length>|<percentage>` offsets it from that edge. An empty positioned inline's `vertical-align` moves the containing block it gives an absolutely positioned child by the same offset it would move a box with content. Text-bearing inline-blocks and the edge-aligned keywords (`top`, `bottom`, `text-top`, `text-bottom`, `middle`) are unchanged.
