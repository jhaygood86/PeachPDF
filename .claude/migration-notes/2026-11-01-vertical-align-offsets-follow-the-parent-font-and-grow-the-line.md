# vertical-align sub/super/middle and raised boxes now match the browsers

Before, `vertical-align: sub` lowered a box by half of its own height and `super` raised it by a fifth of it (so a tall inline-block moved much further than text), `middle` centred the box in the line box, and a box raised or lowered by any offset did not make its line taller, so it could stick out above or below the line (an empty inline behaved the same as text). Now `sub`/`super` move the box by a fifth/a third of the **parent's** font size plus a pixel, `middle` centres it on the parent's baseline plus half the parent's x-height, and the line box grows to hold a raised or lowered box (length, percentage, `sub`, `super`, `middle`, `text-top`, `text-bottom`), with nested offsets adding. A tall empty inline-block also keeps the strut's descent below the baseline. `<sub>` and `<sup>` text sits slightly differently and the lines holding offset content can be taller.
