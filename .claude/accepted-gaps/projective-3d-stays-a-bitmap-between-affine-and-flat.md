# A real perspective stays a bitmap, though plain paths could be projected as vector

HTML boxes and SVG elements alike go through `ProjectiveRaster`: a transform that is projective is painted untransformed into a bitmap and warped through the homography at `RasterizationDpi`; one within a quarter of a device pixel of an affine matrix is a `cm` (vector), and so is a `preserve-3d` context of tilted planes that do not pierce each other (`TryResolveOrder`). Between those, a strong perspective on plain paths could stay vector too (a homography maps lines to lines), but that is a new subsystem: Beziers are not Beziers afterwards, strokes do not map (they would be outlined first), gradients are not invariant (a mesh), text would lose selectability, and filters, masks and images stay raster regardless. Tracked as #1813.

Related, smaller: an HTML context root that paints nothing of its own (a plain `<div style="transform-style: preserve-3d">` holding the planes) still counts as a plane over its whole box, because `SubtreeExtent` always includes the box rectangle. A tilted child crosses that plane at z = 0, so such a context needs the depth-tested bitmap even where its visible planes would not pierce each other. SVG groups have no such rectangle (a group's extent is its content's), so the SVG side is not affected.
