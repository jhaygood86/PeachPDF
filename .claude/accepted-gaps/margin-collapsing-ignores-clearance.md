# Margin collapsing ignores clearance in the adjoining set

Margin collapsing (`CssBox.FoldSelfCollapsingMargins`, added by the Acid2 mouth-gap fix) folds every in-flow descendant's margins of a self-collapsing box into one adjoining set without checking for clearance — CSS2.1 §8.3.1 excludes margins separated by clearance from the adjoining set. Hitting the difference requires a float plus a cleared empty child *inside* a collapse-through box; deemed too exotic to special-case.

`CssBox.FoldOwnAdjoiningBlockEndMargins` — the block-end chain walk, which folds a box's own bottom margin together with its last-in-flow-child chain's — shares the gap for the same reason. §8.3.1 ends its block-end sentence with "…and the child's bottom margin does not collapse with a top margin that has clearance", and the walk does not test for that; its block-start mirror (`FoldOwnAdjoiningBlockStartMargins`) *does* stop at a chain member whose own `clear` is set, so the two sides are deliberately asymmetric here. The block-start check is the cheap one — clearance on the chain member itself — whereas the block-end condition is about clearance on a margin the child's own bottom margin collapsed with further down, which this design does not track.
