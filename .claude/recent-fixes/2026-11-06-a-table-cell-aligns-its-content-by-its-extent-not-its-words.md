# A table cell aligns its content by where it ends, not by where its words end

`CssLayoutEngine.ApplyCellVerticalAlignment` centres (or bottom-aligns) a cell's content in the room it leaves: `(cell far edge - content bottom) / 2`. The content bottom came from `CssBox.GetMaximumBottom`, the lowest of the **words'** rectangles. A word rectangle stops short of three things the cell's own height (from the block layout) does include: the leading under the last line (a line box is `line-height` tall, its text is not), a line that holds only a `<br/>` (no word at all), and the bottom margin of the last block. So a cell with no room to spare was thought to have some, and its content was moved down by half of it. #2185 reported the `<br/>` (`<p>Hello</p><br/>`: the paragraph 8.1pt lower than in Chrome); **measured by dumping the box tree, the same shift happened without the `<br/>`**: the paragraph's own 11.76pt bottom margin made 12.67pt of phantom room and moved it 6.33pt, and the `<br/>` only added a line the measure could not see.

`CellContentBottom` now measures what the cell is made of: the bottom margin edge of its last in-flow block child (`StaticBottom + GetEffectiveBottomMargin()`, the margin that collapses through it too, which is what the height of a box that takes it from its content uses; **only the last** in-flow child, since the margin of an earlier one that a later negative margin swallows is not content: a cell is a formatting context root, so the margins of its children are inside it, CSS 2.1 §8.3.1, and a float it holds is too, §10.6.7; out-of-flow boxes, running boxes and `display: none` are not content, a relatively positioned one counts where it is in the flow, §9.4.3), and the bottom of the lines the cell holds itself, as the line box reaches (`BaselineY + BaselineExtent.BelowBaseline`, leading included) and not as `LineBottom` does (the lowest word, which is all a cell's own line reported: after an alignment its `LineTop` was even **below** its `LineBottom`, because `OffsetOwnLineBoxesTop` moves `FlowTop` and the words' rectangles are what `LineBottom` reads; seen, not looked into). It is taken with the old word-based measure as a maximum, so nothing that was counted stops being counted (which is also why a relatively positioned last child's offset words, and the height of a `display: none` child, still count: both older, see below). Horizontal tables only; a vertical table keeps `GetMaximumRight`.

**Two things the reviews found that the first version got wrong.** (1) The new measure is **clamped to the cell's own far edge**, and a table cell's declared `height` is now **a minimum** for the margin it holds. The engine let a cell with a declared `height` end above its last child's bottom margin (`MarginBottomCollapse` leaves that margin out of a box with a non-auto height, right for a block, wrong for a cell, CSS 2.1 section 17.5.3: the cell grows), which on `main` was invisible because the word measure ignored the margin too; the new measure counted it and the distance came out negative, so the content moved up out of the cell (`<td height=20><p>`: the text above the cell's top, and at a page break a word lost). The cell now grows to hold the margin, as in Chrome, which **changes row heights** for a cell with a declared height shorter than its last block's bottom margin, and the clamp keeps the content from moving past the cell's edge in the shapes the engine still lets overflow (`max-height` on a cell, which a browser ignores). (2) The first version took the largest margin edge over all the cell's block children and the raw `ActualMarginBottom`; a later negative margin then left an earlier child's swallowed margin counted, and a margin collapsing through a nested last child was missed.

What was **not** changed, on purpose: the other two readers of `GetMaximumBottom` in the table engine (a stopped cell's `ActualBottom`, `SpanningCellBandGeometry`). They ask which page band the content *reaches*, not where the cell's content ends, and changing them would move page breaks.

Measured (headless Chrome against PeachPDF, the engine's `main` against this change): the issue's sample puts the text 12.7pt below the table's top with and without the `<br/>` (Chrome 12.8pt, `main` 26.9pt with it). A grid of 576 tables (16 kinds of content, three alignments, a spare-room row, three line heights, two paddings) compared by the text's position inside its own cell: Chrome matched on 189 of 540 for `main` and 345 for this change, 156 improved, none worse (comparing absolute positions in a document of stacked tables showed "worse" cases that were only the tables above drifting). The cells that improve are the ones with a trailing margin, a `<br/>` or a line height above the glyphs; text-only cells with the default line height are unchanged, and `vertical-align: top` is untouched by construction. Of the 240 showcases four changed beyond harness variation (`flexbox`, `print_catalog`, `table_header_repeat`, `text_underline_offset_position`): `flexbox` moves a flex row from 24.8px to 22.3px below its heading, Chrome 22.4px; the others by at most 0.13pt.

Known, left alone (each differs from Chrome without this change): the bottom margin of a block and the margins of an empty block after it are added, not collapsed ([#2252](../accepted-gaps/margins-of-an-empty-block-after-a-block-are-added-not-collapsed.md)); the default margins of `p`, `h2`-`h6`, `blockquote`, lists, `fieldset` and `form` are the HTML 4 ones ([#2251](../accepted-gaps/default-margins-are-the-html-4-ones.md)), which is why a paragraph in a cell is still taller than in Chrome; a `line-height` in `em` is resolved against each descendant's font size instead of inherited as a length ([#2254](../accepted-gaps/line-height-in-em-is-resolved-against-each-descendants-font-size.md): an `h2` with `font-size: 21px` in a cell with `line-height: 1.5em` sits 3.8px lower than in Chrome); a `display: none` child with a `height` still moves a cell's centred content out of it, through the old measure's explicit-height fallback ([#2255](../accepted-gaps/a-display-none-child-with-a-height-moves-a-cells-content-out-of-it.md)); a percentage `line-height` gives zero-height lines everywhere, which makes such a cell 0px tall ([#2253](../accepted-gaps/a-percentage-line-height-gives-every-line-a-height-of-zero.md)).
