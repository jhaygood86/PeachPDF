# A fragment carries its own geometry, so a child splits across a column

_Landed 2026-07-26._

**A fragment carries its own geometry, so a child splits across a column** ([issue #366](https://github.com/jhaygood86/PeachPDF/issues/366), [CSS Fragmentation 3 §2](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#fragmentainer)). #331 moved *when* fragments are produced without changing *where their geometry comes from*: the emitter's per-slot drafts still read `box.Bounds`, `box.Rectangles` and `CssRect.Rectangle`, all of which derive from the box's single `Location`. That is exactly right for pages — every fragmentainer of the page grid differs from the last in the block axis only, and §2 shares one inline size across a box's fragments — and it is why a **column** could hold only whole children: two column-fragments of one box have the same Y range *and* the same X.

**The one idea is that a nested fragmentainer's geometry has to be *stated by layout*, not read back afterwards.** `CssLayoutEngineColumns` hands each column over as it finishes filling it (`FragmentEmitter.RecordNestedFragmentainer`): the column's block band, its inline span, a `BoxGeometrySnapshot` of the children it holds, and — from the break record — which boxes continue past it. Membership becomes a two-axis question (`FragmentRegion`, which is Y-only for a page, so the page path reduces to exactly the old band-overlap rule), and `FragmentKey` gains an instance ordinal so two columns of one slot are distinguishable at all. `BoxGeometrySnapshot` was already the "one box, N positions" precedent (a repeated `<thead>`); this is its second consumer, not a new mechanism.

**The resumed box is moved, which is the half that makes the words right.** `CssBox.ResumeInTheNextFragmentainer` re-places a box continuing into a fragmentainer that has an inline axis of its own — and moving `Location` is the *whole* translation, since `ActualRight`/`ActualBottom` are derived from it (an early version added the delta to `ActualRight` as well, which widened the box by the delta and then moved it again: the paragraph flowed at 225pt in a 105pt column). Its already-placed descendants stay where they are, in the column whose capture holds them.

**The destination is the *lower* of the fragmentainer's content edge and the containing block's, and taking either alone is wrong.** The first draft took the containing block's, on the reasoning that a top-level child of the container shares that coordinate with the column it lands in. That holds only on the page the container *began*: a container spanning pages keeps the top it was placed at there, so on any later page its content edge names a coordinate that page's columns do not reach, and the continuation was sent back to the container's very first column — landing on top of whatever legitimately sat there. The containing block's edge is still needed for the deeper case, where it has already been re-placed inside this fragmentainer and its content edge sits inside its own border and padding. **Found on `windows-latest` only**, as two items reporting *identical* rectangles at the second column's origin in the pre-existing no-overlap invariant (`ContentBeyondTheLastColumn_ResumesOnTheNextPage`); the fixture is knife-edge there, and reproducing it locally took a parameter sweep rather than a re-run — auto-height children instead of `height: 40px` reproduces it on any platform, which is what `AChildContinuingIntoTheNextColumn_OnAPageAfterTheContainersFirst_StaysInThatPagesBand` pins.

**Four defects found by running it, three of them only by the showcase diff.**
(a) **A word after the break still holds the measurement pass's position.** A flow that stops re-places only the words up to the break, so measuring a column's content bottom from words reported the height of the *whole* flow — the container grew a column's worth of blank space below its content. The line boxes the pass kept are the honest source (`CreateLineBoxes` discards the line it stopped in).
(b) **Only a box that positions itself owns its `ActualBottom`** (`PlacesItselfAsBlockBox`, now `internal`). An inline or bare text box holds its previous sibling's coordinates or a line-local value, and reading one inflated the container by ~20pt — which `EngineRelayoutIdempotencyTests` caught as a *non-idempotent* layout, since the bogus value differs between runs. It differs at HEAD too; the guard is what stops it mattering.
(c) **A word on a line the pass discarded must not be captured either.** `AwaitsTheNextFragmentainer` is cleared when the resumed pass re-places the word, so reading the flag at emission time is too late — the snapshot still holds where the word used to be. `BoxGeometrySnapshot` records `List<RPoint?>` and captures null for such a word.
(d) **The balance estimate under-shot by one inter-child gap.** `SimulateWholeChildPacking` tested `height > remaining` while the real fill also has to fit the gap above the child, so the estimate was one gap short, the fill placed one child fewer, and the growth loop bumped the target by 20% — leaving slack a split line then moved into (visible as *unbalanced* columns). Invisible while children were atomic: the slack was unusable.

**And `column-fill: balance` needed an even-share floor to mean anything for splittable content.** The whole-child packing search asks "how tall must a column be for this many *whole* children to fit", which is vacuous for a container holding one long child — a child alone in a column always fits, so the search converges on nothing and the container renders as one tall column. `max(packingEstimate, contentHeight / columnCount)` combines the two: unsplittable children can force a column taller than the even share, never shorter.

Tests: `FragmentEmitterTests` (+3 — a fragment per column at its own inline position, each claiming only its own words with none claimed twice, and a continuing fragment as tall as the content it holds), `MulticolLayoutIntegrationTests` (`AChildIsStillAtomicPerColumn_KnownBoundary` **promoted to the invariant it was drafted as**, plus the resumed box's placement). Full net8.0 suite green (6298); **96% diff coverage**. **62 of 63 showcases are byte-identical**; `multicol` differs, and every difference is an improvement — the dictionary section is now genuinely balanced rather than 5.5/4.5, and it gained a section 6 where one bordered paragraph splits across a column boundary, **verified in both PDFium and MuPDF**. **Accepted gaps:** a split box closes its border on both fragments rather than leaving the break edge open per §6.2 `slice` ([#368](https://github.com/jhaygood86/PeachPDF/issues/368) — a page break gets this right only because the page clip cuts the border away; **now fixed**, see the Recent-fixes entry); and a multicol container nested inside another splits at the outer level only ([#369](https://github.com/jhaygood86/PeachPDF/issues/369) — captures are keyed by pagination slot, and the inner container is re-filled once per outer column).

**Found while sweeping for the defect above, and *pre-existing on `main`*, so deliberately not fixed here:** a `column-fill: balance` retry inside a **resumed** pass throws `An item with the same key has already been added` out of `CssLineBox.AssignRectanglesToBoxes`. `ResetChildrenForRefill` returns early when `resume is not null` — right for the children below the resume point, whose geometry earlier pages' fragments were built from, wrong for the ones at and above it, which this pass is laying out for the first time and which therefore re-produce line boxes they already hold. A fix cannot simply widen the reset: the boundary child is *resumed*, so running its prologue would `RectanglesReset` and blank a fragment an earlier page already painted — what it needs is to discard the lines it produced after its resume ordinal, which is narrower than a reset and has no operation today. Filed as [#374](https://github.com/jhaygood86/PeachPDF/issues/374) — **now fixed**, see the Recent-fixes entry; it is a crash rather than a mis-render, so it ranked above the geometry gaps above.
