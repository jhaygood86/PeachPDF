# §4.3's relaxation ladder stated, and `orphans` decided at the break point

_Landed 2026-07-26._

**§4.3's relaxation ladder stated, and `orphans` decided at the break point** (issues [#305](https://github.com/jhaygood86/PeachPDF/issues/305) and [#301](https://github.com/jhaygood86/PeachPDF/issues/301)'s forward half, [CSS Fragmentation 3 §4.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#possible-breaks)/[§5.4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#widows-orphans)).

**#305's load-bearing idea is that relaxation is a *ladder*, and giving up a constraint whole is only its last rung.** New `Html/Core/Fragmentation/BreakRelaxation.cs` states the tiers once — everything holds, part of the keep-with-next run is left behind, the whole run is, the constraint itself is, break anywhere — and names which existing mechanism owns each, including the driver's own no-progress backstop as §4.3's last resort. `EarlyBreak` records the tier it settled at. The behavioural change is the tier that did not exist: `Discover` **trims the run from its front** until what remains fits the destination, instead of dropping it whole the moment it does not. The members nearest the breaking box are what the chain is about — a subheading immediately above a table matters more than the chapter heading above that — and a run that could not travel at all used to strand both.

**Every tier must keep the decision terminating**, which is why tier 4 leaves a box that fits nowhere alone rather than moving it somewhere it also does not fit: that is #332's 100,000-page runaway, and it is the constraint any new tier inherits.

**#301: `orphans` is knowable at the break point and `widows` is not, and that asymmetry is the whole shape of the fix.** How many lines fall *before* a break is settled the moment the break is taken; how many fall after it is not, since the rest of the content has yet to be flowed. So `orphans` becomes an ordinary break decision — `CssBox.LayoutBlockChildren` reads `InlineBreakToken.LinesKeptHere` (new: the cumulative `CompletedLineCount` cannot answer this for any fragment but the first) and converts a break *inside* a child that kept too few lines into a break *before* it, which is `KeptNothingInThisFragmentainer` generalized from §4.4's zero-line case to §5.4's. `widows` stays the retroactive whole-box push, and that is now the only half of the arm that matters.

**Two more guards, both about a decision that must not repeat.** The conversion is taken **at most once per box per layout** — not per pass: the parent's child loop runs again every pass, and a box that travelled with its keep-with-next run lands the same distance below the fragmentainer top it did before, so repeated it walks the box down the document against a 100,000-pass cap. And the conversion is skipped where nothing precedes the box in this fragmentainer, because moving it then cannot give it more room — it would keep the same too-few lines at the top of the next one and ask again. That is tier 4 (give the constraint up) rather than tier 1, and without it a band too small for `orphans` lines walks the box down the document one page at a time (measured; `i > start` does not cover it, since a zero-height preceding sibling satisfies that while leaving the box at the band top).

**Two things this got wrong first, both worth not re-deriving.** (a) **The target has to travel with the break.** A break-before whose top is left to be re-derived places the child at its natural position again — still inside the fragmentainer being left, for a box that kept a line or two — so the resumed pass reaches the same conclusion and the driver's backstop is what ends it. The zero-line case got away without a target because such a box's own top is already past the boundary. (b) **The run travels here too.** Six keep-with-next tests failed until the orphans break routed through `EarlyBreak.Discover` + `TryRestartAt` like the §3.1-propagation branch beside it: the reason the break moved is different, but "do not strand the heading above it" is the same requirement, and this is the only level the run's members are siblings at.

Tests: `OrphansWidowsIntegrationTests` (+4 — a paragraph **taller than the band** now breaks before itself rather than stranding one line, which is exactly the case `TallParagraph_ExceedsOnePage_IsNotNudged` documents the push as unable to help; `orphans: 1` keeping its single-line fragment; the natural break left alone, and the no-room-above case), a `FragmentEmitterTests` case pinning that the page the box broke from keeps no ghost fragment of it, `KeepWithNextIntegrationTests` (+1, run trimming, verified load-bearing by reverting `continue` to `break` — it fails). Full net8.0 suite green (6304); **100% diff coverage**. **62 of 63 showcases byte-identical**; `aspect_ratio` differs, and it differs by *losing* an interior gap — a two-line paragraph the retroactive push had relocated with the fragmentainer gap inside it now has the break fall before it and lays out clean, **verified in both PDFium and MuPDF**. **Accepted gaps:** the orphans mover is inert in a document with **per-page left/right `@page` margins** ([#373](https://github.com/jhaygood86/PeachPDF/issues/373)) — a box's page is provisional while the reflow loop is settling, and a break moved from a provisional assignment stopped that loop converging *on `windows-latest` only*, leaving a paragraph wrapped to a neighbouring page's measure (found by CI, not locally: the font metrics put the fixture at a different boundary). And per-line `widows` selection needs the driver to be able to re-run an *earlier* fragmentainer pass with a line budget, since the total line count is only known once the box completes — filed as [#371](https://github.com/jhaygood86/PeachPDF/issues/371), and #301 stays open for it. (**#371 is now fixed** — see the Recent-fixes entry; the pass to re-enter turned out to be the one that just *finished*, not an earlier one, because in block flow the box that stopped is the last content in its fragmentainer.)
