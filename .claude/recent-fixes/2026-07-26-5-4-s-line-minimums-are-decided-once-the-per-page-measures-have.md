# §5.4's line minimums are decided once the per-page measures have settled

_Landed 2026-07-26._

**§5.4's line minimums are decided once the per-page measures have settled** ([issue #373](https://github.com/jhaygood86/PeachPDF/issues/373), [CSS Fragmentation 3 §5.4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#widows-orphans) against CSS Paged Media 3 §5). Both halves of §5.4 were gated off in a document with per-page left/right `@page` margins, because a break moved from a *provisional* page assignment feeds back into the loop that is settling it — measured on `windows-latest` as a document that stopped converging within its cap, leaving a paragraph wrapped to a neighbouring page's measure.

**The load-bearing idea is that the feedback is a property of the loop, not of the decision.** #373 offered two routes — make the loop converge with break decisions in it, or take the decision after it settles — and the second is now nearly free: the loop already ends on a stable box→page signature, so **one more layout after it** has no feedback at all. Every width in that layout comes from the settled assignment, and nothing runs afterwards to be disturbed by what the corrections move. `HtmlContainerInt.PageWidthsSettled` is the gate (`!UseVariablePageWidth || settled`), so a document without per-page horizontal margins is unchanged: it has no loop and nothing provisional, and both minimums apply from its first pass as they always have.

**A correction may not move a box onto a page of a different measure, and that guard is the half this got wrong first.** The final layout cannot re-wrap what it moves, so such a correction leaves the box wrapped for the page it left — the very defect the reflow loop exists to remove. It was written, argued away on the grounds that the retroactive whole-box push makes the same trade already, and **put back when CI failed on `windows-latest`**: a block wrapped to a full-bleed first page's 812pt measure, sitting on a 412pt page, failing the pre-existing `ManyParagraphsAcrossPages_ReflowConverges_EachBlockOwnPageMeasure`. The argument was half right — the push *is* the mover that did it, and it had never been gated — so the guard is asked of **all three** §5.4 movers: the orphans break conversion, the per-line `widows` rewind, and the whole-box push. (Mirrored inner/outer margins are *not* an instance: the two pages' content widths are equal and only the left origin differs, and it is the width the wrap depends on.)

**What actually changes is the *quality* of the correction, not whether one happens** — which is why the fixture that shows it has to distinguish the two mechanisms. An `orphans` violation was already relocated by the whole-box push, reaching the same page; the per-line `widows` correction is the one that differs, keeping the box where flow put it and moving only the line it takes. `WidowsViolation_MovesTheLinesItTakes_RatherThanTheWholeParagraph` is therefore the load-bearing test (verified by reverting the gate), on a 400×100 sheet so a page boundary falls inside a four-line paragraph — the geometry `OrphansWidowsIntegrationTests` already calibrates against. The document-scale test beside it asserts the invariant instead: every block still carries its own page's measure, and no block straddles a boundary short of its minimums.

Tests: `PerPageHorizontalReflowLayoutIntegrationTests` (+2, and the former `OrphansViolation_DoesNotMoveABreak_WhilePageWidthsAreStillSettling` **promoted** from pinning the gap to pinning the invariant, as `OrphansAndWidows_AreEnforced_WithoutDisturbingThePerPageMeasure`). Full net8.0 suite green (6369); **100% diff coverage**. **64 of 65 showcases are byte-identical**; `paged_media_horizontal_reflow` goes from **8 pages to 7** — exactly the improvement #373 predicted, a paragraph that had been alone on a near-empty final page joining the one before it. Verified in both PDFium and MuPDF.
